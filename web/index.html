About these files

Vector76's strut plate generator, ported to the browser and checked against the original OpenSCAD output at 58 sizes. 1/4" MDF, 1/8" bit (3.5 mm dogbones), 1" EMT wings (11.5 mm). The DXF is closed polylines in mm. The G-code follows V1E's milling basics: bolt holes first, then each outline with tabs, 0.25 mm into the spoil board. Check it in a G-code viewer before you cut.

FluidNC config

About this config

V1 Engineering's Jackpot3 LowRider config (FluidNC_Configs, JP3_LR, AGPL-3.0, read from GitHub when you click) with the X and Y max_travel_mm set for this machine: (home switch to the far stop, less pulloff_mm: FluidNC counts the travel from the pulled-off spot). Everything else is V1E's (Z travel, homing, pins, motor currents). Or bring your own config.yaml and only its two travel lines change. Check it against your machine before use.

FluidNC: the real firmware, built to run in your browser, drives the machine with the config.yaml in use (V1E's JP3_LR until you load your own, under Build): jog it, home it ($H on the machine's switches), send it G-code. It runs a copy with plain stepper drivers ($CD shows that copy).

About this model

The machine is rebuilt from the measured FreeCAD model: every part's position was fitted across 13 FreeCAD builds from 15 x 30 in to 48 x 96 in (3 to 8 braces) and checked part by part against each, and against builds left out of the fit (within 0.1 mm).

Fixed for now: 1" EMT (29.5 mm), 6.5 mm XZ plates, the Makita / Kobalt compact router mount. Approximate: Jackpot3 lid, power supply, router colours, the X belt's end loop screws, the vacuum hose route.

LowRider 4 parts by V1 Engineering, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; strut plates by Vector76. Personal use; not affiliated with V1 Engineering. The pendant: the FluidTouch case and stand, MIT. "Report a problem" opens a GitHub issue with your size and file checksums filled in.
